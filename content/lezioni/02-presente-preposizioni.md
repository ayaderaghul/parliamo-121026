# 2. Present tense and prepositions

Covers 15 nodes: `verb.essere.present`, `verb.avere.present`, `pron.subject`,
`verb.present.regular`, `verb.present.isc`, `verb.present.irregular`,
`verb.modal`, `negation.non`, `interrogative`, `prep.simple`,
`prep.articulated`, `partitive`, `esserci`, `demonstrative`, `possessive`.

---

### essere, present · `verb.essere.present` · A1
*Prerequisites: none (root)*

*sono, sei, è, siamo, siete, sono*

Note that *sono* is both **io** and **loro**; context or a subject pronoun
disambiguates. *è* always carries the accent — *e* without it means "and", and
this is the most common written error in all of Italian.

Uses: identity and origin (*sono italiana*), permanent and temporary states
(*sono stanca*), time (*sono le tre*), and later the auxiliary for one class of
verbs (→ `aux.selection`) and the passive (→ `passivo`).

Italian does not split *essere* into two verbs the way Spanish and Portuguese do.
Speakers of those languages transfer *estar* onto *stare*, which in Italian is
much narrower: location of people (*sto a casa*), health (*sto bene*),
and the progressive (*sto mangiando*).

---

### avere, present · `verb.avere.present` · A1
*Prerequisites: none (root)*

*ho, hai, ha, abbiamo, avete, hanno*

The **h** is silent and purely orthographic, distinguishing *ho* from *o* ("or"),
*hai* from *ai*, *ha* from *a*, *hanno* from *anno* ("year"). It is never
pronounced and never optional in writing.

**Idiomatic uses where English uses "to be":** *ho fame, ho sete, ho sonno,
ho freddo, ho caldo, ho paura, ho fretta, ho ragione, ho torto, ho vent'anni.*
These are high-frequency and worth drilling as a block, because
*sono ventisette anni* and *sono fame* are persistent transfer errors.

### Subject pronouns · `pron.subject` · A1
*Prerequisites: none (root)*

*io, tu, lui/lei, noi, voi, loro.* Formal address uses **Lei** with third-person
singular verbs: *Lei è italiano?*

The important fact is that they are normally **omitted**. The verb ending already
identifies the subject, so *parlo italiano* is the neutral sentence and
*io parlo italiano* means "*I* speak Italian (as opposed to someone else)".

**Trap.** English and French speakers use them constantly, which doesn't sound
wrong so much as insistent and slightly childish. Use them only for contrast
(*io lavoro, lui no*), for emphasis, after *anche* (*anch'io*), or to
disambiguate identical forms (*io ero / lui era* is fine without, but the
subjunctive *che io/tu/lui parli* genuinely needs them).

### Regular present · `verb.present.regular` · A1
*Prerequisites: `verb.essere.present`, `phon.stress`*

Three conjugation classes, defined by the infinitive ending.

| | -are (parlare) | -ere (credere) | -ire (dormire) |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | parl**o** | cred**o** | dorm**o** |
| tu | parl**i** | cred**i** | dorm**i** |
| lui/lei | parl**a** | cred**e** | dorm**e** |
| noi | parl**iamo** | cred**iamo** | dorm**iamo** |
| voi | parl**ate** | cred**ete** | dorm**ite** |
| loro | parl**ano** | cred**ono** | dorm**ono** |

Stress falls on the stem everywhere except *noi* and *voi*: *PARlo, PARli, PARla,
parLIAmo, parLAte, PARlano.*

**Spelling adjustments in -are verbs** (sound preservation, not irregularity):
*cercare → cerchi, cerchiamo*; *pagare → paghi, paghiamo*;
*cominciare → cominci* (one i, not *comincii*); *mangiare → mangi, mangiamo*;
*studiare → studi*.

**Trap.** Italian has one present tense where English has three. *Parlo italiano*
covers "I speak", "I am speaking" and "I do speak". The progressive
*sto parlando* exists (→ `gerundio`) but is reserved for genuinely
in-progress-right-now, far narrower than English "-ing".

The highest out-degree node in the graph: eleven other nodes depend on it.

### -isc- verbs · `verb.present.isc` · A1
*Prerequisites: `verb.present.regular`*

A large subgroup of **-ire** verbs inserts **-isc-** in the singular and third
plural:

*fin**isc**o, fin**isc**i, fin**isc**e, finiamo, finite, fin**isc**ono*

Note the sound shift the spelling hides: *finisco* is hard, *finisci/finisce* are
soft.

Common members: *finire, capire, preferire, pulire, costruire, spedire,
suggerire, unire, restituire, colpire.*
Common non-members: *dormire, aprire, partire, sentire, offrire, seguire,
servire, soffrire.*

**Trap.** Membership is lexical and unpredictable from meaning or form. It must be
learned per verb, which is exactly why this is a separate node rather than a
footnote to the regular present.

### Irregular present · `verb.present.irregular` · A1
*Prerequisites: `verb.present.regular`*

The irregulars are the most frequent verbs in the language, so they must be
automatic.

| | andare | fare | stare | dare | venire | uscire | dire | sapere |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| io | vado | faccio | sto | do | vengo | esco | dico | so |
| tu | vai | fai | stai | dai | vieni | esci | dici | sai |
| lui | va | fa | sta | dà | viene | esce | dice | sa |
| noi | andiamo | facciamo | stiamo | diamo | veniamo | usciamo | diciamo | sappiamo |
| voi | andate | fate | state | date | venite | uscite | dite | sapete |
| loro | vanno | fanno | stanno | danno | vengono | escono | dicono | sanno |

Also frequent: *bere* (bevo, bevi, beve, beviamo, bevete, bevono),
*rimanere* (rimango... rimangono), *tenere* (tengo... tengono),
*scegliere* (scelgo... scelgono), *salire* (salgo... salgono).

**Pattern worth naming:** many irregulars show a **-g-** in *io* and *loro* only:
*vengo/vengono, tengo/tengono, salgo/salgono, rimango/rimangono, scelgo/scelgono*.
Recognising the pattern converts five memorisations into one.

Note *dà* (gives) carries an accent to distinguish it from *da* (from).

### potere, volere, dovere · `verb.modal` · A1
*Prerequisites: `verb.present.irregular`*

| | potere | volere | dovere |
|---|---|---|---|
| io | posso | voglio | devo |
| tu | puoi | vuoi | devi |
| lui | può | vuole | deve |
| noi | possiamo | vogliamo | dobbiamo |
| voi | potete | volete | dovete |
| loro | possono | vogliono | devono |

They take a bare infinitive with no preposition: *posso entrare*, *voglio
mangiare*, *devo partire*. Inserting *di* or *a* is a common transfer error.

**Consequences later.** Modals interact with two nodes downstream:
clitics can attach to the infinitive or precede the modal
(*lo devo fare / devo farlo*, → `clitic.placement`), and in the past they
normally inherit the auxiliary of the following infinitive
(*ho dovuto mangiare* but *sono dovuto partire*, → `aux.selection`).

*volere* + noun means "want" (*voglio un caffè*); *volerci* means "to take/require"
(*ci vuole un'ora*, → `pron.ci`).

### Negation · `negation.non` · A1
*Prerequisites: `verb.present.regular`*

Put **non** immediately before the verb: *non parlo italiano*, *non ho capito*.
There is no auxiliary "do", no inversion, no *pas*.

**Double negation is required, not forbidden.** When a negative word follows the
verb, *non* stays:

*Non ho visto **nessuno**. Non c'è **niente**. Non vado **mai** al cinema.
Non ho **più** fame. Non è **ancora** arrivato. Non mangio **né** carne **né**
pesce.*

If the negative word comes first, *non* disappears: ***Nessuno** ha capito.*
***Niente** è cambiato.* ***Mai** dire mai.*

**Trap.** English speakers delete the second negative (*ho visto nessuno*), and
speakers of languages with single negation do the same. With clitics, *non* goes
first of all: *non lo so*, *non ci sono mai stato*, *non gliel'ho detto*.

### Question words · `interrogative` · A1
*Prerequisites: `verb.present.regular`*

*chi, che / che cosa / cosa, quando, dove, come, perché, quanto, quale.*

Yes/no questions need **no change at all** — only intonation in speech and a
question mark in writing: *Parli italiano?*

**Points worth stating explicitly:**
- *quale* agrees in number (*quale libro, quali libri*) and elides before *è*:
  *qual è* — never *qual'è*.
- *quanto* agrees fully when it quantifies a noun: *quanti anni, quante volte*,
  but is invariable as an adverb: *quanto costa*.
- *perché* means both "why" and "because", so *perché non vieni? perché lavoro*.
- *dove* elides before *è*: *dov'è*. *come* likewise: *com'è*.
- *che* + noun asks "which": *che libro leggi?*

**Trap.** Subject-verb inversion is optional and unmarked in Italian:
*Dove vai?* and *Dove tu vai?* are both understandable but the second is
unnatural; the subject, if present, usually goes to the end: *Dove va Marco?*

### Simple prepositions · `prep.simple` · A1
*Prerequisites: none (root)*

*di, a, da, in, con, su, per, tra/fra.*

Rough core senses, though usage is heavily lexical:

- **di** possession, material, topic: *il libro di Marco, di legno, parlo di te*
- **a** destination and location for cities, indirect object, time:
  *vado a Roma, a Marco, alle tre*
- **da** origin, agent, "at someone's place", purpose:
  *vengo da Milano, scritto da lei, vado dal medico, occhiali da sole*
- **in** location and destination for countries, regions, enclosed spaces:
  *in Italia, in banca, in macchina*
- **su** on, about: *sul tavolo, un libro sulla storia*
- **per** for, through, in order to: *per te, per Roma, per studiare*
- **tra/fra** between, among, in (future time): *tra noi, tra due ore* — the two
  are interchangeable and chosen for euphony (*fra tre* rather than *tra tre*)

**Traps.** *a* with cities but *in* with countries: *a Roma, in Italia*. *da* with
people meaning "to/at their place": *vado da mia sorella*. Verb-governed
prepositions are lexical and must be learned with the verb
(→ `infinito.usi`). This is the quietest long-term error source in the language,
because wrong prepositions almost never block comprehension.

### Articulated prepositions · `prep.articulated` · A1
*Prerequisites: `prep.simple`, `article.definite`*

Five prepositions fuse obligatorily with the definite article.

| | il | lo | l' | la | i | gli | le |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **di** | del | dello | dell' | della | dei | degli | delle |
| **a** | al | allo | all' | alla | ai | agli | alle |
| **da** | dal | dallo | dall' | dalla | dai | dagli | dalle |
| **in** | nel | nello | nell' | nella | nei | negli | nelle |
| **su** | sul | sullo | sull' | sulla | sui | sugli | sulle |

*con* optionally fuses (*col, coi* — informal); *per, tra, fra* never do.

**Traps.** The fusion is obligatory: *di il* and *a la* do not exist. But it only
happens with the *definite* article — *in una casa*, *di un amico* stay separate.
And the article appears more often than English suggests, so *vado in banca*
(no article, institutional) contrasts with *vado nella banca di fronte*.

---

### Partitive · `partitive` · A2
*Prerequisites: `prep.articulated`*

*di* + definite article expresses "some / a few / any", and serves as the plural
of the indefinite article.

*Vorrei **del** pane. Ho comprato **della** frutta. Ci sono **dei** problemi.
Ho visto **degli** amici.*

Alternatives: **qualche** + *singular* noun (*qualche amico* = some friends),
**alcuni/alcune** + *plural* (*alcuni amici*), **un po' di** for uncountables
(*un po' di pane*).

**Traps.** *qualche* is always followed by a singular noun despite its plural
meaning — *qualche amici* is wrong. In negative sentences the partitive is
normally dropped: *non ho **pane***, not *non ho del pane*. This node is the
prerequisite for pronominal **ne** (→ `pron.ne`), which is the partitive turned
into a pronoun.

### c'è, ci sono · `esserci` · A1
*Prerequisites: `verb.essere.present`, `noun.number`*

Existential "there is / there are", and it **agrees with what follows**:
*c'è un problema*, *ci sono molti problemi*.

Past: *c'era / c'erano* (imperfect, description) and *c'è stato / ci sono stati*
(perfect, event).

**Traps.** Learners freeze *c'è* as invariable and produce *c'è molte persone*.
Distinguish from *è*: *c'è* asserts existence, *è* identifies (*c'è un libro sul
tavolo* vs *il libro è sul tavolo*). Note *ecco* is not the same thing: it points
(*ecco il libro!* = here it is).

### questo, quello · `demonstrative` · A1
*Prerequisites: `adj.agreement`, `article.definite`*

**questo** (near) is a regular four-form adjective: *questo, questa, questi,
queste*; it may elide before a vowel (*quest'anno*).

**quello** (far) inflects exactly like the definite article:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| quel libro | quei libri | (il / i) |
| quello studente | quegli studenti | (lo / gli) |
| quell'amico | quegli amici | (l' / gli) |
| quella casa | quelle case | (la / le) |

Standing alone as a pronoun, it keeps the full form: *voglio **quello***,
*preferisco **quelli***.

**Trap.** *quello* as an adjective is never *quello libro*; the article-like
reduction is obligatory. This is a good diagnostic item: getting it right shows
the *il/lo/l'* system is real rather than memorised word by word.

### Possessives · `possessive` · A1
*Prerequisites: `article.definite`, `adj.agreement`*

| | m.sg | f.sg | m.pl | f.pl |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| my | il mio | la mia | i miei | le mie |
| your | il tuo | la tua | i tuoi | le tue |
| his/her/its | il suo | la sua | i suoi | le sue |
| our | il nostro | la nostra | i nostri | le nostre |
| your (pl) | il vostro | la vostra | i vostri | le vostre |
| their | il loro | la loro | i loro | le loro |

Two rules that surprise English speakers:

1. **The article is part of the possessive.** *La mia casa*, not *mia casa*.
2. **Agreement is with the thing possessed, not the owner.** *Il suo libro* is
   "his book" or "her book" — Italian does not distinguish. *La sua macchina* said
   of a man is correct.

**Exception:** singular, unmodified family members drop the article — *mia madre,
tuo fratello, sua sorella*. The article returns in the plural (*i miei
fratelli*), with modifiers (*la mia sorella maggiore*), with diminutives
(*la mia sorellina*), and always with *loro* (*il loro padre*).

**Trap.** *loro* is invariable: *il loro libro, la loro casa, i loro libri*.
Italian also omits possessives where the owner is obvious, especially with body
parts and clothing: *mi lavo **le** mani*, not *lavo le mie mani*.
