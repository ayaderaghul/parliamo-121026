# 3. The clitic pronoun system

Covers 9 nodes: `pron.reflexive`, `verb.reflexive`, `pron.direct`,
`pron.indirect`, `verb.piacere`, `pron.ne`, `pron.ci`, `pron.combined`,
`clitic.placement`.

This is one of the three spines of the language. It is also where learners who
otherwise sound fluent give themselves away, because clitics are unstressed,
fast, and never strictly necessary for comprehension.

---

### Reflexive pronouns · `pron.reflexive` · A1
*Prerequisites: `verb.present.regular`*

*mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si* — placed before the verb.

*Io **mi** lavo. Tu **ti** chiami Marco. Loro **si** vedono ogni giorno.*

Note *si* serves both third person singular and plural, and *ci/vi* are shared
with the direct and indirect series, which is why the same form can mean
different things in different sentences.

---

### Reflexive verbs · `verb.reflexive` · A1
*Prerequisites: `pron.reflexive`, `verb.present.irregular`*

Listed in dictionaries with **-si**: *chiamarsi, alzarsi, svegliarsi, vestirsi,
divertirsi, annoiarsi, arrabbiarsi, ricordarsi, accorgersi, sentirsi.*

Conjugation is ordinary, with the pronoun in front:
*mi alzo, ti alzi, si alza, ci alziamo, vi alzate, si alzano.*

Three distinct uses:

- **True reflexive** — the subject acts on itself: *mi lavo*.
- **Reciprocal** — plural subjects acting on each other: *si scrivono ogni
  settimana*, *ci vediamo domani*.
- **Inherent / pronominal** — no reflexive meaning at all, the pronoun is simply
  part of the verb: *mi ricordo, mi accorgo, mi arrabbio, mi vergogno*. These
  have to be learned as units.

**Consequence.** All reflexives take **essere** in compound tenses, with
participle agreement: *mi sono alzat**a***, *si sono divertit**i***. Many learners
who handle *mi alzo* fluently still produce *mi ho alzato* — the node is easy in
the present and hard in the past, which is why `aux.selection` sits downstream.

**Trap.** Italian uses reflexives with body parts and clothing where English uses
a possessive: *mi lavo le mani*, *si mette la giacca*. There's also a "benefactive"
use with no translation: *mi mangio una pizza*, *si è fatto un caffè* — roughly
"treat oneself to".

### Direct object pronouns · `pron.direct` · A2
*Prerequisites: `verb.present.regular`, `noun.gender`*

*mi, ti, **lo/la**, ci, vi, **li/le***

They replace a noun that answers "whom / what", with no preposition:
*Conosci Maria? Sì, **la** conosco.* *Compri i libri? Sì, **li** compro.*

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| lo | m. sg | *il libro → **lo** leggo* |
| la | f. sg | *la rivista → **la** leggo* |
| li | m. pl | *i libri → **li** leggo* |
| le | f. pl | *le riviste → **le** leggo* |

*lo* and *la* elide before a vowel or *h*: *l'ho visto*, *l'aspetto*. *li* and
*le* **never** elide: *li ho visti*, not *l'ho visti*.

**Invariable lo.** Neuter *lo* stands for a whole clause: *Lo so. Non lo credo.
Te lo dico io.*

**Trap.** Because the pronoun carries gender, every unstable gender assignment
resurfaces here. Learners who say *la libro* only once a month will say *la
leggo* about a masculine noun far more often, because the noun isn't there to
remind them.

### Indirect object pronouns · `pron.indirect` · A2
*Prerequisites: `pron.direct`, `prep.simple`*

*mi, ti, **gli/le**, ci, vi, **gli*** (*loro* in formal writing)

They replace **a** + person: *a Marco → **gli***, *a Maria → **le***,
*ai ragazzi → **gli***.

*Ho telefonato a Marco → **gli** ho telefonato.*
*Ho scritto a Maria → **le** ho scritto.*

The whole difficulty is deciding which series a verb takes. Verbs that take an
indirect object in Italian: *telefonare, parlare, scrivere, dire, chiedere,
rispondere, dare, regalare, mandare, piacere, sembrare, servire, mancare,
bastare, interessare*.

**Traps.**
- *le* is feminine singular indirect, but also feminine plural **direct**. Same
  form, opposite roles: *le ho scritto* (to her) vs *le ho viste* (them, f.).
- Verbs that mismatch English: *telefonare a qualcuno* (indirect, where English
  "call someone" is direct), *chiedere a qualcuno*, *rispondere a qualcuno*.
  Conversely *ascoltare, guardare, aspettare, cercare* are direct in Italian
  where English inserts a preposition: *lo ascolto*, *la aspetto*.
- Spoken Italian increasingly uses *gli* for feminine and plural too. It is
  widespread and neostandard, but still marked in writing.

### piacere and similar verbs · `verb.piacere` · A1
*Prerequisites: `pron.indirect`*

The structure is inverted relative to English. The thing liked is the
**subject**; the person is the **indirect object**.

*Mi piace il libro.* — literally "to me is pleasing the book."
*Mi piacciono i libri.* — plural subject, so plural verb.

So there are only two forms in normal use, *piace* and *piacciono*, and the
choice is decided by the thing, not the person. With an infinitive, always
singular: *mi piace leggere*.

To emphasise or contrast the person, use the stressed form: *A me piace, a lui no.*

Same pattern: *mancare* (*mi manchi* = I miss you — note the reversal),
*servire* (*mi serve una penna*), *bastare* (*mi bastano due giorni*),
*interessare, sembrare, restare, succedere, dispiacere* (*mi dispiace*).

**Trap and structural note.** *Io piaccio la pizza* is the classic fossilised
error. It survives for years because *mi piace* is taught at A1 as a fixed
phrase, while the grammar it depends on (indirect pronouns) arrives at A2.
Expect learners to have the phrase and not the structure, and to break the moment
they need *a lui piacciono* or *gli è piaciuto*.

In compound tenses the auxiliary is **essere** and the participle agrees with the
subject: *mi è piaciut**a** la festa*, *mi sono piaciut**i** i libri*.

### The pronoun ne · `pron.ne` · A2
*Prerequisites: `partitive`, `pron.direct`*

**ne** replaces *di* + something, or a quantified portion.

- **Partitive** — "of them": *Quante mele vuoi? **Ne** voglio tre.*
  *Hai fratelli? Sì, **ne** ho due.*
- **di + topic**: *Parliamo del film → **ne** parliamo.*
  *Che **ne** pensi?*
- **di + place, origin**: *Vengo dalla Francia → **ne** vengo.*
- **Idiomatic**: *non **ne** posso più* (I can't take it any more),
  *me **ne** vado* (I'm off), *ne vale la pena* (it's worth it).

**Trap.** *ne* is obligatory with a bare number or quantity. *Ho tre* is not
Italian; it must be *ne ho tre*. English speakers drop it constantly because
"I have three" is complete in English.

In the past, the participle agrees with what *ne* refers to:
*Di mele, **ne** ho mangiat**e** tre.*

---

### The pronoun ci · `pron.ci` · A2
*Prerequisites: `esserci`, `pron.direct`*

Three jobs, plus a fourth as a plain pronoun.

- **Locative** — replaces a place: *Vai a Roma? Sì, **ci** vado.*
  *Sei stato in Francia? **Ci** sono stato due volte.*
- **a + thing** after certain verbs: *Pensi al lavoro? **Ci** penso.*
  *Credi ai fantasmi? Non **ci** credo.*
- **Existential** in *c'è / ci sono*.
- **Us**, direct or indirect object: *ci vedono, ci hanno scritto*.

**Verbs where ci fuses into the meaning:** *volerci* (to take, of time or
resources: *ci vuole un'ora*, *ci vogliono due ore* — agrees with what's needed),
*metterci* (to take, with a subject: *ci metto un'ora*), *entrarci* (to be
relevant: *non c'entra niente*), *farcela* (to manage: *non ce la faccio*),
*starci* (to be up for it: *ci sto*).

**Trap.** *ci vuole* and *ci metto* both translate "it takes", and the difference
is who's in the sentence: *ci vogliono due ore* (impersonal) vs *ci metto due ore*
(I take two hours). Note *ci* becomes *ce* before another clitic (*ce ne sono*).

### Combined pronouns · `pron.combined` · B1
*Prerequisites: `pron.direct`, `pron.indirect`, `pron.ne`*

When two clitics meet, the **indirect comes first** and **-i becomes -e**;
third-person indirect *gli/le* both become **glie-** and fuse into one word.

| | + lo | + la | + li | + le | + ne |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mi | me lo | me la | me li | me le | me ne |
| ti | te lo | te la | te li | te le | te ne |
| gli/le | glielo | gliela | glieli | gliele | gliene |
| ci | ce lo | ce la | ce li | ce le | ce ne |
| vi | ve lo | ve la | ve li | ve le | ve ne |

*Me lo dai? Te lo spiego domani. Gliel'ho già detto. Ce ne sono tre.*

**Traps.** *glielo* is a single word and serves masculine, feminine and plural
recipients alike — there is no *lelo*. Before a vowel, only *lo/la* elide:
*gliel'ho detto*, but *glieli ho dati*.

This node is a reliable diagnostic. A learner who has genuinely internalised the
clitic system produces *glieli* under time pressure; one who has memorised
phrases will stall here.

### Clitic placement · `clitic.placement` · B1
*Prerequisites: `pron.direct`, `verb.modal`*

**Before the conjugated verb** by default: *lo vedo, gliel'ho detto, non ci vado.*

**Attached to the end** (enclitic, written as one word) with:

- infinitives — *voglio veder**lo***, *prima di far**lo*** (infinitive drops its
  final -e)
- gerunds — *veden**dolo***, *andando**sene***
- informal imperatives — *dim**mi***, *fal**lo***, *andiamo**ci***, *dite**gli***
- *ecco* — *ecco**mi***, *eccolo*

**With modals, both positions are correct:** *lo devo fare* = *devo farlo*;
*te lo posso dire* = *posso dirtelo*. No difference in meaning or register.

**Traps.**
- Monosyllabic imperatives double the following consonant: *da' + mi → **dammi***,
  *fa' + lo → **fallo***, *di' + gli → **digli***, *va' + ci → **vacci***.
  Exception: *gli* never doubles — *digli*, not *diggli*.
- Negative imperative with *tu* uses the infinitive, and the clitic can go either
  side: *non farlo* or *non lo fare*.
- With formal *Lei*, clitics go **before**: *mi dica*, *si accomodi* — never
  *dicami*.
