# 7. Nodes added after the first lesson pass

Covers 5 nodes: `adj.bello.buono`, `pron.stressed`, `verb.progressive`,
`adverb.position`, `piacere.formula`. Same five-part shape as the other files.

---

### Bello e buono prenominali · `adj.bello.buono` · A1
*Prerequisites: `adj.agreement`, `article.definite`*

Before a noun, these two adjectives stop behaving like adjectives and inflect
like articles.

**bello** follows the definite article:

| | singular | plural |
|---|---|---|
| before most consonants | **bel** film | **bei** film |
| before s+cons., z, gn, ps | **bello** zaino | **begli** zaini |
| before a vowel | **bell'**amico | **begli** amici |
| feminine | **bella** casa | **belle** case |

**buono** follows the indefinite article: *un **buon** giorno, **buona** sera,
**buon'**amica, **buono** studente.* In the plural it is regular:
*buoni amici, buone idee.*

**Traps.** After the noun or after *essere*, both are ordinary adjectives again:
*un film **bello***, *il film è **bello***, *questo caffè è **buono***. The
reduced forms only exist in front. *Che bello!* standing alone is also the full
form.

### Pronomi tonici · `pron.stressed` · A1
*Prerequisites: `pron.subject`, `prep.simple`*

*me, te, lui, lei, noi, voi, loro* — the stressed series, used wherever a
pronoun has to stand on its own.

Three jobs:

- **After a preposition:** *con **me**, per **te**, secondo **lui**, da **noi**,
  senza di **te***. Clitics cannot do this at all, which is why this is a
  separate skill and not a variant of `pron.direct`.
- **Contrast and emphasis:** *A **me** piace, a **lui** no.* *Ha invitato **te**,
  non me.*
- **Comparisons:** *è più alto di **me***, *come **te***.

**Traps.** *me* and *te*, not *io* and *tu*, after prepositions: *con me*, never
*con io*. Note *da me* means "at my place" as well as "from me". And *secondo me*
is the ordinary way to say "in my opinion" — far more frequent than *penso che*,
and it takes the indicative, so it lets an A1 learner express opinions long
before the subjunctive exists.

### Stare + gerundio · `verb.progressive` · A2
*Prerequisites: `gerundio`, `verb.present.irregular`*

*stare* conjugated + the gerund: *sto mangiando, stai leggendo, stiamo uscendo*.
Past: *stavo dormendo*.

The whole difficulty is that it is **much narrower than English "-ing"**. It
means in progress at this exact moment, and nothing else.

| Italian | English |
|---|---|
| *Sto mangiando.* | I'm eating (right now) |
| *Lavoro in banca.* | I'm working at a bank (these months) |
| *Domani vado a Roma.* | I'm going to Rome tomorrow |
| *Studio italiano da due anni.* | I've been studying Italian for two years |

**Traps.** Only the last three are the error: English speakers reach for
*sto lavorando in banca* and *sto andando a Roma domani*, which are both wrong.
There is no future progressive. Clitics attach to the gerund or precede *stare*:
*lo sto facendo* = *sto facendolo*. And *stare per* + infinitive is a different
construction meaning imminence: *sto per uscire*, I'm about to go out.

### Posizione degli avverbi · `adverb.position` · A2
*Prerequisites: `pp.avere`, `negation.non`*

In compound tenses, a specific set of adverbs goes **between the auxiliary and
the participle**: *sempre, già, mai, più, appena, ancora, anche, quasi*.

*Ho **sempre** pensato che fosse difficile.*
*Non ho **mai** visto una cosa simile.*
*È **appena** arrivato.*
*Non ci sono **più** tornato.*
*Hai **già** mangiato?*

**Traps.** Placing them after the participle (*ho pensato sempre*, *non sono
stato mai*) is marked rather than impossible, which is exactly why it fossilises:
nobody corrects it and it never blocks meaning. With *non*, the order is fixed:
*non* + auxiliary + adverb + participle.

Other adverbs behave differently. Most manner adverbs follow the participle:
*ha parlato **lentamente***, *ha risposto **gentilmente***. Time adverbials go at
either end: ***ieri** ho visto Marco* / *ho visto Marco **ieri***.

### Mi piace, mi piacciono · `piacere.formula` · A1
*Prerequisites: `noun.number`*

The week-two formula, and the only productive choice inside it is singular
versus plural:

*Mi **piace** il gelato.* — one thing
*Mi **piacciono** i gelati.* — more than one
*Mi **piace** leggere.* — an infinitive is always singular

The verb agrees with the **thing**, not with the person. Change the person and
the verb doesn't move: *ti piace, gli piace, le piace, ci piace, vi piace*.

Negative: *non mi piace*. Past: *mi è piaciut**o** il film*, *mi sono
piaciut**e** le foto* — *essere*, with agreement.

**Traps.** *Io piaccio la pizza* is the error, and it comes from mapping the
English subject onto the Italian one. The formula alone won't prevent it,
because the formula has no visible structure to generalise from. That comes at
`verb.piacere`, where the person becomes a real indirect object and the whole
family (*mancare, servire, bastare, interessare, sembrare*) opens up.

Worth teaching as a phrase early and revisiting as grammar later, which is
exactly what the two separate nodes encode.
