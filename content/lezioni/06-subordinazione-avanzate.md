### Subordinate clauses with che · `subordination.che` · A2
*Prerequisites: `verb.present.regular`*

*che* introduces a subordinate clause and, unlike English "that", is **never
omitted**: *Penso **che** hai ragione*, never *penso hai ragione*.

**Same subject → di + infinitive instead:**
*Penso **di** avere ragione.* *Spero **di** vederti.* *Credo **di** capire.*
Compare *Penso che **tu** abbia ragione* (different subject, and a subjunctive
trigger).

Verbs taking *di* + infinitive: *pensare, credere, sperare, dire, decidere,
cercare, finire, smettere*. Verbs taking *a*: *cominciare, continuare, riuscire,
imparare, provare, aiutare*. This is lexical and belongs with `infinito.usi`.

### Relative pronouns che, cui · `relative.che.cui` · B1
*Prerequisites: `subordination.che`, `prep.simple`*

**che** is invariable and covers both subject and direct object:
*Il libro **che** ho letto. La ragazza **che** abita qui.*

**cui** is used after a preposition:
*La persona **a cui** ho scritto. Il motivo **per cui** sono venuto.
La città **in cui** vivo. L'amico **di cui** ti ho parlato.*

**il cui / la cui** expresses possession ("whose"), and the article agrees with
the **thing possessed**, not the owner:
*Il libro, **la cui** copertina è rossa, è mio.*
*Lo scrittore, **i cui** romanzi conosci, è morto.*

**Traps.**
- *che* is never used after a preposition. *La persona che ho scritto* is wrong;
  it must be *a cui*.
- Unlike English, the relative can never be dropped: *il libro che ho letto*,
  never *il libro ho letto*.
- *dove* can replace *in cui* for places: *la città dove vivo*.

### il quale, ciò che · `relative.ilquale` · B2
*Prerequisites: `relative.che.cui`, `article.definite`*

**il quale / la quale / i quali / le quali** is a formal alternative to
*che* and *cui*. Because it agrees, it disambiguates where *che* cannot:

*La sorella di Marco, **la quale** abita a Roma...* — clearly the sister, not
Marco.

It combines with prepositions in articulated form: *del quale, al quale,
nel quale, sul quale*.

**ciò che / quello che / quel che** = "what" in the sense of "that which":
*Non capisco **ciò che** dici. **Quello che** conta è la salute.*
After *tutto*: *tutto **quello che** so*, *tutto **ciò che** ho*.

**Trap.** *ciò che* and *quello che* are interchangeable; *ciò* alone is formal
and written. *Che cosa* is the question word and cannot do this job:
*non capisco che cosa dici* is a different, more marked construction.

### Textual connectives · `connettivi` · B1
*Prerequisites: `subordination.che`*

The move from B1 to B2 is largely the move beyond *e, ma, perché, quindi*.

**Concession (+ subjunctive):** *benché, sebbene, nonostante, malgrado,
per quanto* — *Benché **fosse** tardi, siamo usciti.*
Also *anche se* (+ **indicative**): *anche se era tardi*.
**Purpose (+ subjunctive):** *affinché, perché* (purpose), *in modo che*.
**Condition (+ subjunctive):** *purché, a condizione che, a meno che (non),
qualora*.
**Time:** *mentre, appena, non appena, prima che* (+subj) / *prima di* (+inf),
*dopo che, finché*.
**Cause:** *perché, siccome, poiché, dato che, visto che* (all + indicative).
**Consequence:** *quindi, perciò, dunque, di conseguenza, così... che,
talmente... che*.
**Adversative:** *tuttavia, però, eppure, anzi, invece, al contrario,
d'altra parte*.
**Discourse organisation:** *innanzitutto, inoltre, del resto, per quanto
riguarda, infatti, in effetti, insomma, comunque*.

**Traps.** *anzi* means "on the contrary / in fact even more so", not "anyway".
*Infatti* confirms rather than contrasts ("indeed"), and is not English "in
fact" used to correct. *Siccome* can only open a sentence; *perché* cannot.

### Sequence of tenses · `concordanza.tempi` · B1
*Prerequisites: `congiuntivo.imperfetto`, `condizionale.passato`*

The subordinate tense is chosen relative to the main clause.

**Main clause in the present:**

| relation | subordinate |
|---|---|
| same time | *Penso che **sia** stanco.* |
| earlier | *Penso che **sia stato** stanco.* |
| later | *Penso che **sarà** stanco.* |

**Main clause in the past or conditional:**

| relation | subordinate |
|---|---|
| same time | *Pensavo che **fosse** stanco.* |
| earlier | *Pensavo che **fosse stato** stanco.* |
| later | *Pensavo che **sarebbe stato** stanco.* |

The bottom-right cell is the one that breaks people: "later than a past moment"
is the **condizionale passato**, not the present conditional.

**Indicative equivalents** work the same way: *Ha detto che **veniva** /
**era venuto** / **sarebbe venuto***.

### Reported speech · `discorso.indiretto` · B2
*Prerequisites: `concordanza.tempi`, `trapassato`*

When the reporting verb is in the past, everything shifts back.

| direct | reported |
|---|---|
| presente | imperfetto |
| passato prossimo | trapassato |
| futuro | condizionale passato |
| imperativo | *di* + infinito |
| congiuntivo presente | congiuntivo imperfetto |

*«Sono stanco» → Ha detto che **era** stanco.*
*«Ho finito» → Ha detto che **aveva finito**.*
*«Domani parto» → Ha detto che **sarebbe partito** il giorno dopo.*
*«Vieni qui!» → Mi ha detto **di venire**.*

**Deictics shift too:** *ora → allora*, *oggi → quel giorno*,
*domani → il giorno dopo*, *ieri → il giorno prima*, *qui → lì*,
*questo → quello*, and person pronouns adjust.

**Trap and teaching note.** The components (`condizionale.passato`,
`trapassato`, `concordanza.tempi`) are usually already solid when this fails.
The failure is at assembly: too many simultaneous shifts under time pressure.
The treatment is not re-explanation but transformation drills — twenty
direct-to-reported conversions until the backshift is automatic rather than
computed.

### First conditional · `ipotetico.1` · A2
*Prerequisites: `futuro.semplice`, `subordination.che`*

Real, possible conditions. **Indicative in both clauses.**

*Se **ho** tempo, **vengo**.*
*Se **avrò** tempo, **verrò**.*
*Se **piove**, **resto** a casa.*
*Se non **ti sbrighi**, **perderemo** il treno.*

**Trap.** *se* is **never** followed by the present conditional or the present
subjunctive. *Se avrei tempo* and *se abbia tempo* are both impossible. This
single restriction prevents most conditional errors, and it's teachable at A2,
long before the subjunctive exists.

---

### Second conditional · `ipotetico.2` · B1
*Prerequisites: `congiuntivo.imperfetto`, `condizionale.presente`*

Unreal or unlikely present conditions.

**se + congiuntivo imperfetto → condizionale presente**

*Se **avessi** tempo, **verrei** volentieri.*
*Se **fossi** in te, non lo **farei**.*
*Cosa **faresti** se **vincessi** la lotteria?*

A two-parent convergence: it cannot be taught before both halves exist, and
teaching it earlier is why so many learners produce *se avrei*.

**Trap.** English "if I had" looks like a past tense, which encourages *se avevo*.
Italian requires the subjunctive here. The imperfect indicative does appear in
casual speech (*se avevo tempo, venivo*), but it's colloquial and best taught
after the standard form is secure.

---

### Third conditional · `ipotetico.3` · B2
*Prerequisites: `congiuntivo.trapassato`, `condizionale.passato`*

Unreal past: what would have happened but didn't.

**se + congiuntivo trapassato → condizionale passato**

*Se lo **avessi saputo**, non **sarei venuto**.*
*Se **fossimo partiti** prima, **avremmo evitato** il traffico.*

**Mixed conditionals** are frequent and worth teaching alongside: a past cause
with a present result takes the present conditional in the main clause —
*Se **avessi studiato**, ora **saprei** l'italiano.*

### si impersonale and si passivante · `si.impersonale` · B1
*Prerequisites: `pron.reflexive`, `verb.present.regular`*

Two constructions that look identical and behave differently.

**si impersonale** — no specific subject, "one / people / you in general".
The verb is **always third singular**:
*In Italia **si mangia** bene. **Si dice** che... Come **si dice** in italiano?
Qui non **si fuma**.*

**si passivante** — a passive with a real subject, which the verb **agrees with**:
*Qui **si vende** la casa.* → *Qui **si vendono** le case.*
***Si cerca** un cameriere.* → ***Si cercano** camerieri.*

The test: if there's a direct object that could be the subject, the verb agrees
with it.

**Traps.**
- In compound tenses the impersonal takes *essere* with a masculine plural
  participle: *si è mangiat**o** bene*, but *si è andat**i** al cinema*.
- Adjectives after impersonal *si* go masculine plural: *quando **si è
  stanchi**...*
- With an already-reflexive verb, *si* doubles as *ci si*: ***ci si** alza presto*.

### Passive voice · `passivo` · B1
*Prerequisites: `verb.participio.regular`, `adj.agreement`, `aux.selection`*

**essere** + participle, with the participle agreeing with the subject, and the
agent introduced by **da**:

*La lettera **è stata scritta** da Maria.*
*Le case **sono state costruite** nel 1950.*

**venire** can replace *essere* in simple tenses, and emphasises the process
rather than the resulting state: *La porta **viene chiusa** alle otto*
(it gets closed) vs *La porta **è chiusa*** (it is closed — ambiguous between
passive and adjective). *venire* is impossible in compound tenses.

**andare** + participle expresses obligation: *Il modulo **va compilato** in
stampatello* (must be filled in). Very common in instructions and bureaucracy.

**Trap.** Italian prefers alternatives to the passive far more than English does:
*si passivante* (*si vendono case*) and an impersonal third plural
(*hanno rubato la macchina* = the car was stolen). Overusing the passive is one
of the clearest markers of translated-sounding Italian.

### Gerund · `gerundio` · A2
*Prerequisites: `verb.present.regular`*

Formed with **-ando** (-are) and **-endo** (-ere, -ire): *parlando, credendo,
dormendo*. Irregulars: *facendo, dicendo, bevendo*.

**Progressive** with *stare*: *Sto **mangiando**. Stavo **leggendo**.*
This is genuinely in-progress-right-now only. Habits and general present take the
plain present: *lavoro in banca*, not *sto lavorando in banca*.

**Adverbial gerund** — expresses time, cause, means or manner, with the **same
subject** as the main clause:
*Tornando a casa, ho incontrato Luca.* (while returning)
*Essendo stanco, sono rimasto a casa.* (since I was tired)
*Si impara **sbagliando**.* (by making mistakes)

**Traps.**
- Clitics attach to the gerund: *vedendolo, andandosene*.
- The subject must match. *Tornando a casa, ha cominciato a piovere* is an error;
  use *mentre tornavo a casa*.
- English "-ing" after a preposition is an **infinitive** in Italian:
  *prima di **partire***, *senza **dire** niente*, *dopo **aver mangiato***.
  Never *prima di partendo*.

### Uses of the infinitive · `infinito.usi` · B1
*Prerequisites: `prep.simple`, `verb.modal`*

The infinitive is the verb's noun form, and the main difficulty is which
preposition governs it.

**No preposition:** modals (*posso fare*), *sapere* (*so nuotare*),
*preferire, desiderare, piacere*, impersonals (*bisogna fare*), and verbs of
perception (*ho visto passare*).
**di:** *cercare, finire, smettere, decidere, dimenticare, ricordarsi, sperare,
credere, pensare, avere bisogno/paura/voglia*.
**a:** *cominciare, continuare, riuscire, imparare, provare, aiutare, andare,
venire, insegnare, abituarsi*.
**per** (purpose): *studio **per** imparare*.

**Infinito sostantivato** — as a noun, with an article: *il mangiare, il dover
lavorare*.
**Infinito passato** — after *dopo*: ***dopo aver mangiato***, *dopo essere
arrivata* (agreeing, with essere verbs).

**Trap.** Preposition choice is lexical, not logical, and never blocks
comprehension, so it fossilises silently. It's an excellent candidate for
collocation-style drilling rather than rule teaching (→ C-level model).

### Comparatives · `comparativo` · A2
*Prerequisites: `adj.agreement`, `prep.simple`*

**più / meno ... di** before nouns, pronouns and numbers:
*Marco è **più alto di** Luca. Ho **meno di** venti euro.*

**più / meno ... che** when comparing two qualities, two verbs, two nouns after a
preposition, or two elements of the same kind in one clause:
*È **più** simpatico **che** intelligente.*
*Meglio tardi **che** mai.*
*Ci sono **più** turisti a Roma **che** a Milano.*

**Equality:** *(tanto)... quanto*, *(così)... come*:
*È alto **quanto** me. Non è **così** difficile **come** sembra.*

**Irregular comparatives:** *buono → migliore*, *cattivo → peggiore*,
*grande → maggiore*, *piccolo → minore*; adverbs *bene → meglio*,
*male → peggio*.

**Traps.** *di* + article contracts: *più alto **del** fratello*. The *di/che*
split is the whole difficulty of this node and the reason it deserves its own
diagnostic item. *migliore* is an adjective, *meglio* an adverb:
*questo vino è **migliore***, but *si sta **meglio*** — confusing them is one of
the most common intermediate errors.

### Superlatives · `superlativo` · A2
*Prerequisites: `comparativo`, `article.definite`*

**Relative** — definite article + *più/meno*, with **di** for the group:
*È **il** ragazzo **più** alto **della** classe.*
*Sono **le** città **meno** care **d'**Italia.*

**Absolute** — *-issimo*, agreeing fully: *bellissimo, bellissima, bellissimi,
bellissime*. Or *molto* + adjective. Or, colloquially, reduplication
(*piano piano*) or prefixes (*stra-, super-*).

Irregular absolutes: *buono → ottimo*, *cattivo → pessimo*,
*grande → massimo*, *piccolo → minimo*.

**Traps.** Spelling: *ricco → ricchissimo*, *lungo → lunghissimo*,
*simpatico → simpaticissimo*. The article is not repeated when the adjective
follows the noun with its own article already present: *il ragazzo più alto*,
not *il ragazzo il più alto* — a common French-speaker transfer. Note the
relative superlative triggers the subjunctive in a following relative clause:
*è il film più bello che **abbia** visto*.

### Adverbs in -mente · `avverbi.mente` · A2
*Prerequisites: `adj.agreement`*

Formed from the **feminine singular** of the adjective + *-mente*:
*lento → lenta → lenta**mente***; *chiaro → chiara**mente***.
Two-form adjectives add it to the base: *veloce → veloce**mente***.

Adjectives in *-le* and *-re* drop the final *-e*: *facile → facil**mente***,
*regolare → regolar**mente***, *gentile → gentil**mente***.

**Traps.** Gender surfaces yet again here, which is why this node hangs off
`adj.agreement`. Note some adverbs are irregular or suppletive: *bene, male,
meglio, peggio, volentieri*. And several adjectives double as adverbs:
*parla **piano***, *costa **caro***, *lavora **sodo***, *vai **dritto***.
Italian often prefers a prepositional phrase to a long *-mente* adverb:
*con calma*, *in fretta*, *di solito*.

### Pronominal verbs · `verbi.pronominali` · B2
*Prerequisites: `pron.combined`, `clitic.placement`, `pron.ci`*

Verbs fused with one or two clitics whose meaning is not predictable from the
parts. High frequency, opaque, and almost never taught systematically.

| verb | meaning | example |
|---|---|---|
| *farcela* | to manage | *Non **ce la** faccio più.* |
| *andarsene* | to leave, be off | ***Me ne** vado.* |
| *cavarsela* | to get by | *Se **la** cava bene in italiano.* |
| *metterci* | to take (time) | ***Ci** metto un'ora.* |
| *volerci* | to be needed | ***Ci** vogliono due ore.* |
| *entrarci* | to be relevant | *Non **c'**entra niente.* |
| *avercela con* | to be annoyed with | ***Ce l'**ha con me.* |
| *prendersela* | to take offence | *Non **te la** prendere.* |
| *fregarsene* | not to care (vulgar-ish) | *Non **me ne** frega niente.* |
| *smetterla* | to stop it | ***Smettila**!* |

**Note.** These conjugate as normal verbs with the clitics moving as usual:
*me ne sono andato*, *ce l'ho fatta*, *non ce la facevo*. The participle of
*farcela* and *cavarsela* agrees with the *la*: *ce l'ho fatt**a***.

### Register and formality · `registro` · B2
*Prerequisites: `imperativo.formal`, `condizionale.presente`*

Not a rule but a control layer: the same content across three registers.

**Formal (Lei, written and institutional):**
*Gentile Dottoressa, **Le invio** in allegato... **Resto a disposizione** per
qualsiasi chiarimento. **Distinti saluti**.*
Markers: *Lei*, conditional for requests (*Potrebbe...*), *si prega di*,
*in merito a*, *qualora*, formal imperative, passives and nominalisations.

**Neutral (tu, everyday polite):**
*Ciao Marco, **ti mando** il documento. **Fammi sapere**.*

**Colloquial:** *boh* (no idea), *mica* (not at all: *non è mica facile*),
*magari* (maybe / if only), *tipo* (like), *cioè*, *insomma*, *dai*,
*che palle*, *un casino* (a mess / a lot).

**Two cultural notes that matter more than grammar.** Switching from *Lei* to
*tu* is a negotiated move, usually offered by the older or senior person
(*possiamo darci del tu?*). And Italian bureaucratic and professional writing is
considerably more formal than its English equivalent — an email that would read
as warm in English can read as brusque in Italian without the conditional and
the closing formula.

This node is where correctness stops being the measure and appropriateness takes
over, which is the transition into the C-level model.
